## Why a VP is grinding NeetCode

Two honest reasons.

**First, the day job does not exercise it.** Platform work at a million users a day is about failure modes, release safety, component design, and accessibility. It is excellent practice for almost everything . and it keeps no graph algorithms sharp at all. Skills you do not use decay, and the decay is invisible until you need them.

**Second, committing it publicly makes it harder to quietly abandon.** [neetcode-submissions](../projects/neetcode-submissions.md "NeetCode.io submissions across the DSA and SQL tracks.") is a learning log with a commit history. A private scratch folder is a promise; a public repository is a record.

## What is in it

- **Data structures & algorithms** . including **Dijkstra's shortest path**. He also has a standalone `Djakstras_Algorithm` repository from 2020, so this is the second pass . which is the point of maintenance.
- **SQL** . the NeetCode SQL track.

## How he practises

1. **Write it, commit it, move on.** The goal is reps, not a perfect solution file.
2. **Repeat the ones that went badly.** The Dijkstra implementation exists twice, six years apart.
3. **Keep it visible.** A log nobody can see is a log you can skip.

## The honest framing

This is **maintenance, not preparation**. The repository is a public commitment device for a skill the job does not touch, and it is a good pattern for anybody senior enough that nobody is checking their fundamentals any more.
