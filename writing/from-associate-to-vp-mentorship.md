## The one-line version

> Code quality and delivery velocity went up when the investment went into **people** rather than process. Some of it worked. Some of it did not, and that part is in the post too.

## The timeline that prompted it

**Associate Software Engineer, March 2019. Vice President, February 2023.** Under four years, and the honest explanation is not talent . it is that [one platform got owned properly](../systems/singledoor-payment-platform.md "SingleDoor . BillPay, QuickPay with Zelle, and card payments.") for long enough to see it break, fix it, and still be there for the consequences.

Longer answer: [How did Ali go from Associate to VP in under four years?](../faq/how-ali-reached-vp-in-under-four-years.md)

## What raising the bar actually took

Not a process change. The things that moved the numbers were unglamorous:

- **Review for the reason, not the diff.** The useful review question is "what is this trying to do?" . not "is this line correct?"
- **Give people the whole problem.** An engineer handed a well-specified ticket learns to implement. An engineer handed a problem learns to decide.
- **Make the standard visible.** [Accessibility in the definition of done](../systems/accessibility-definition-of-done.md "Keyboard coverage, focus order, contrast, screen-reader smoke test, per story.") worked because it was written down and applied to every story . including the inconvenient ones.
- **Remove the bottleneck you are.** If the team waits on you to deploy, to approve, or to know something, that is a problem with your work, not theirs. [Feature-flag self-service](../systems/feature-flag-self-service.md "Every engineer controls their own rollout.") came out of exactly that realisation.

## And the part that did not work

Scheduled mentorship. Standing one-on-ones with no problem attached produce status updates, not growth. The sessions that mattered were the ones that happened **next to a real piece of work** . a review, an incident, a design that was not quite right yet.

Mentorship is not a meeting. It is a thing you do while doing something else.
