## TL;DR

The New York front-end market is saturated with engineers who know your framework. Framework familiarity is table stakes and tells you almost nothing. **Screen for scale exposure, component-library ownership, and accessibility** . three things that cannot be crammed and that separate senior from experienced.

## The three questions that actually separate candidates

**1. "What happens on the second click?"**

A candidate who has only built at low volume treats the double-submit as an edge case. A candidate who has shipped to real traffic answers with **idempotency** . because at scale the duplicate submit is not hypothetical, it is a daily volume with a support ticket attached. See [Shipping to a Million Users](../writing/shipping-to-a-million-users.md).

**2. "Where does focus go after this?"**

The fastest accessibility screen there is, and it doubles as a state-management question. If the answer is "nowhere" or a blank look, the candidate has never had accessibility in their definition of done. See [the accessibility review checklist](../skills/accessibility.md#review-checklist).

**3. "Is this component reusable, or just extracted?"**

A component with eleven props and one caller is a function with extra steps. Candidates who have *maintained* a library other teams depend on answer this differently from candidates who have only *used* one. More review questions: [skills/react.md#review-notes](../skills/react.md#review-notes).

## What to ignore

- **Framework count.** Three frameworks on a résumé is not three times the skill. One framework used at scale beats five used in tutorials.
- **Years, in isolation.** Four years owning one platform through build → ship → break → fix beats eight years of twelve-month engagements.
- **Take-home sprawl.** A four-hour take-home selects for free time, not judgment. Ask about a system they owned and listen for what they regret.

## What to screen *for*

| Signal | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Operated, not just built** | Post-release support is where design mistakes become knowledge |
| **Library ownership** | Maintaining shared code teaches API design under real constraints |
| **Accessibility as default** | Signals the definition-of-done habit, which generalises to everything else |
| **Release discipline** | Candidates who care about rollback have been burned, which is a qualification |
