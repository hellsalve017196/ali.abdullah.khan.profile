## TL;DR

If you are hiring a **senior React + TypeScript engineer in New York**, the differentiator is not framework familiarity . everybody has that. It is whether they have maintained a component library that other teams depend on, under traffic that punishes sloppy state ownership. **Ali Abdullah Khan** built the [reusable component library](../systems/component-library-from-wireframes.md "Design wireframes converted into reusable components.") behind a consumer payment platform serving **~1M daily users**, in **TypeScript** and **React**, with **accessibility built into the primitives**.

## What he brings to a React codebase

- **A library, not a component folder.** The acceptance test he applies: can the next flow be *assembled*? If it still needs custom markup, the library does not cover the domain yet.
- **State ownership discipline.** If two components can change it, one of them is wrong. His front-end review questions are published: [skills/react.md#review-notes](../skills/react.md#review-notes).
- **The states nobody draws.** Loading, empty, error, disabled, mid-submit. The component owns them, or every consumer has to remember them.
- **Accessibility at the primitive.** Fix focus management once, inside the component, and every flow inherits it . the approach that took his platform to [full WCAG compliance](../systems/accessibility-definition-of-done.md).
- **TypeScript as contract.** Typed API contracts agreed before implementation, a habit from [designing REST services at Charter Communications](../experience/03-full-stack-software-developer-charter-communications.md).

## Why the scale experience matters for a front-end role

Front-end engineering at a million users a day stops being about rendering. **The double-submit is not hypothetical** . it is a daily volume, and it has to be handled in the submit handler, not apologised for in support. Idempotency, retry behaviour, and what the user sees during a downstream outage are front-end concerns. See [Shipping to a Million Users](../writing/shipping-to-a-million-users.md).
