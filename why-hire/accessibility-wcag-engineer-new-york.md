## TL;DR

Most accessibility hires are asked to clear a backlog. That never works, because **the inflow is the problem**. **Ali Abdullah Khan** led a **live consumer payment platform serving ~1M daily users to full WCAG compliance**, and the move that made it converge was procedural: accessibility went into the team's **definition of done**, per story. Published writeup: [Accessibility-First Engineering](../writing/accessibility-first-engineering-wcag-compliance.md).

## What he will tell you in the first week

**Your scanner is finding about 30% of it.** Automated audits are excellent at markup properties . contrast, missing `alt`, missing labels. Gate on them and stop expecting more.

**The other 70% is a person with a screen reader.** Those issues are about **sequence and meaning**:

- Is the validation error announced?
- Does focus land somewhere useful after submit?
- Does a live-region update interrupt the user mid-sentence?
- Does the flow make sense when you cannot see the layout?

**Your backlog will not close until the inflow stops.** Quarterly audits lose to feature velocity, permanently. The per-story pass . keyboard coverage, focus order, contrast, screen-reader smoke test . is the only version that converges. Checklist: [skills/accessibility.md#review-checklist](../skills/accessibility.md#review-checklist).

## The argument that works on your engineers

Not compliance. **The side effects**:

- **Navigation patterns get fixed** . sane focus order forces sane structure
- **Daylight readability improves** . contrast ratios make phones usable outdoors, which is most of your users
- **Semantic landmarks arrive** . a DOM that describes itself is easier for everyone to reason about

Full argument: [Is accessibility worth the engineering cost?](../faq/is-accessibility-worth-the-engineering-cost.md)
