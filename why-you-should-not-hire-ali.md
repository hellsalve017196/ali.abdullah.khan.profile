# Why You Should **Not** Hire Ali

### [← Back](README.md) . [Profile](ali-abdullah-khan-profile.md)

Every profile page is written to persuade. This one is written to disqualify, because a bad fit costs both sides a quarter and the honest version is cheaper.

## 1. He is a platform engineer, not a greenfield prototyper

If you need an MVP in six weeks and you do not yet know whether anyone wants it, **he is the wrong hire**. He will ask about rollback before he asks about the feature. He will want to know what the thing does when a dependency is down. On a product that may not exist in three months, those questions are overhead.

Hire someone who likes throwing code away. Ali has spent seven years on a platform where you cannot.

## 2. He will slow you down at the specification stage

He writes [PRDs before code](learning/spec-first-development-with-ai-agents.md "A repository with a specification and no implementation is a project that has not yet earned its code.") . including on hobby projects. One of his public repositories is a [household expense tracker with a full spec and no implementation at all](projects/taka-koi.md).

If your culture is "ship first, figure out what it was for later", that habit will read as friction. It is friction. It is deliberate.

## 3. He will make accessibility your problem

He [took a live platform to full WCAG compliance](systems/accessibility-definition-of-done.md "Keyboard coverage, focus order, contrast, screen-reader smoke test . per story.") and he is not going to quietly drop that standard to hit a date. Expect him to argue that a story is not done, and to keep arguing.

If accessibility is something you intend to address "after launch", do not hire him into that argument. You will both lose time and he will probably win.

## 4. He has gone deep, not wide

**Seven years, one employer, one domain.** If you want someone who has seen twelve architectures at twelve companies, that is not his résumé. His depth is **consumer payments at scale, front-end platform, release engineering, accessibility**. Outside that, he is a competent generalist . not an expert, and he will tell you which is which.

## 5. He is not available for consulting

He is **full-time at JP Morgan Chase**. [working-with-ali.md](working-with-ali.md "Mentorship, code review, accessibility audits, AI-workflow setup, speaking.") is a short list of things he makes time for, not a services menu. If you are looking to contract a team, this is the wrong profile.

## 6. He is skeptical about AI tooling, in a specific way

He built an [AI-driven SDLC](systems/ai-driven-sdlc.md) and he will still tell you that **most AI adoption produces faster typing**, that the workflow degrades the moment the knowledge base goes stale, and that **maintaining the vault is the job**.

If you want someone to tell leadership that the tool will make the team 40% faster, hire somebody else. He will ask who is maintaining the context.

## 7. Long Island

He is in **Holbrook, New York**. Fully remote works; NYC via LIRR works. **Relocating does not** . see [Long Island teams](why-hire/why-ali-abdullah-khan-is-best-in-long-island.md).
